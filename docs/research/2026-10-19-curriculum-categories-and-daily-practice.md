## Research conclusion

Use the supplied category list as the product's navigable catalogue, with explicit, independently identifiable learning objectives underneath it. Keep curriculum alignment, practice category, presentation format and dated assignment as separate concepts. Adam needs the assigned slice of that catalogue, not a catalogue browser. The daily-only presentation is the user's requirement and already appears in [weekly practice](../product/weekly-practice.md); external curriculum guidance does not prescribe that interface.

The four product subjects are **Rekenen, Taal, Spelling and Begrijpend Lezen**. They need not duplicate the canonical curriculum structure: Rekenen belongs to Rekenen/wiskunde; the other three are product views of Nederlands. SLO's older content-line overview separates Nederlands into oral language, language awareness/care, reading and writing, which also shows why a product menu is not the curriculum itself. [SLO, Inhoudslijnen Nederlands](https://www.slo.nl/sectoren/po/inhoudslijnen-po/inhoudslijnen-nederlands/).

## Catalogue distinctions to preserve

These are modelling recommendations inferred from the attachment and sources, not new educational approval:

- **Subject/category:** the guardian-facing organization, such as Rekenen → Optellen or Rekenen → Redactiesommen.
- **Learning objective:** a bounded capability, such as addition through a hundred boundary within a stated number range. Give it one stable identity even when it appears under several practice categories.
- **Practice format:** numerical response, finite text, choice, dictation or a multi-step explanation. An existing approved renderer can serve multiple categories; a new catalogue entry does not imply a new screen.
- **Content attributes:** story context, reading topic/type, numeric range, spelling pattern, required operation and reviewed difficulty. These support selection without pretending to be all the same taxonomy level.
- **Dated task:** the selected questions or reading bundle, task identity, category label and pinned objective/content references. Selection happens before the child sees today's work.

Specific overlaps worth preserving are Hoofdrekenen with operation categories, Geld/Tijd/Meten with Redactiesommen, word formation with spelling, and vocabulary with reading in context. Tags can describe overlap; future evidence attribution must use the actual assessed response, not grant mastery to every associated tag. Written/oral language capabilities from the attachment remain valid catalogue scope, but current short-answer/choice previews do not implement or assess all of them.

## Recommended integration

### A simple catalogue over shared objectives

Use four product subjects and the supplied category organization. Keep one primary browsing parent for categories; use explicit objective/category relationships for legitimate overlap. Do not build separate copies of a skill under every menu path. Meaning matters: word formation and correct spelling can have different observable objectives even when their labels overlap.

A category says what the guardian selected. The objective says what an individual question can actually assess. A format describes how the exercise is presented or answered. Those are different fields. For example, Rekenen → Redactiesommen → Geldsommen may target interpreting a money situation and subtracting amounts; a final numeric answer alone cannot independently prove every associated skill. Use one primary assessed objective unless separate observed steps support more; associated/context skills do not gain automatic mastery credit.

Stable competency identifiers and explicit relationships are also consistent with the [1EdTech CASE 1.1 implementation guide](https://www.imsglobal.org/spec/CASE/v1p1/impl), which supports linking content and results to identifiable competencies across frameworks. This supports the existing architecture's direction; it is not a recommendation to install CASE services, build an integration or claim conformance now.

Recommended minimal local representation for the next slice:

- A typed catalogue with stable IDs, product subject/category hierarchy, objective references and supported-preview coverage. Start in the existing practice module; avoid empty future modules or dependencies.
- Explicit task assignment metadata: stable instance ID, date/block reference, primary display category plus any intentionally mixed category targets, objective revisions, pinned question sequence or whole reading bundle, and presentation/response kind.
- Question-to-objective links where questions assess different objectives; labels and category tags are not grading or mastery rules.
- Existing snapshot map remains keyed by task instance ID. Two tasks for the same category, or the same category on two dates, do not share progress. Relabelling a category never resets answers. Later durable assignments must pin curriculum/content versions rather than changing historical meaning during edits.
- Unsupported catalogue entries stay visibly unavailable for assignment until their content and response/evaluation method exist. Adding “Klokkijken”, “Schrijven” or “Spreken en luisteren” to the catalogue must not route them through today's generic numeric/text graders.

The full list can be represented as catalogue scope without pretending every category has working lessons. Existing approved arithmetic, finite-text, story and reading compositions are reusable across compatible categories. Clock/geometry interactions, dictation/audio, open writing and oral assessment need their own bounded designs and suitable evaluation policies. No automatic AI grading is implied by the attachment.

### Guardian plan versus Adam's daily view

The confirmed manual repeating plan remains guardian-owned. Its selection path becomes **weekday → block → subject → category → selected learning objective(s) → quantity**, with existing guardian-controlled difficulty. Categories narrow the selection; they do not authorize an automatic planner to choose a new schedule. The full catalogue and coverage status belong in Weekplanning/Beheer, not on Adam's home screen.

The plan is a reusable configuration. A dated assignment is its concrete occurrence with pinned tasks/content. Adam receives only today's assigned block instances, in guardian order; within the current block he retains the already-confirmed choice of task order. He does not browse all categories or get a subject-selection screen. Completed blocks can still show their status for that day. Work from another date appears only when explicitly carried into today's assignments under the approved future policy.

Task numbering may remain secondary where several tasks share a label. It must not replace the category or serve as the state identifier. The reading example's category labels would require matching reviewed question metadata; the existing mixed bundle must show its actual targets rather than pretend to test only these two. Mixed practice is explicitly labelled with its selected categories, not silently squeezed into one leaf category or represented as a new subject.

Guardian results should reuse the same metadata and retain all actual attempts. Later category/skill reporting can summarize the relevant evidence, but a task score is not mastery. One task listed in several related views must still count once in block completion and subject totals.

### Options considered

1. **Keep five hard-coded screen/subject definitions and add a category string:** fastest label patch, but still conflates curriculum, assignment and renderer, hides mixed content, and cannot reliably select today's work.
2. **Create a distinct screen, grader and independent skill tree for every category:** duplicates the approved compositions and learning facts; cannot handle word-problem/reading overlap well.
3. **Recommended: explicit assignment metadata + a simple catalogue + shared objectives and existing compositions:** gives meaningful task labels now, fits the approved architecture and permits broader content coverage in later bounded slices. The next prototype does not require the full future curriculum schema.
