# Nexai: reliable motion for the 24 approved poses

19 October 2026. Primary-source research for the user's request to give every approved pose a small animation. This report recommends a method; it does not approve animation or educational event assignments.

## Correction after user review

The user rejected whole-image transforms: the intended small animation means a visible change inside the character, such as blinking or a moving paw. The original recommendation below misinterpreted that requirement and is superseded.

The replacement reuses the proven master greeting technique: a fixed approved pose plus separately registered eye patches, with independent left/right opacity. The new eye source is generated as an edit of each exact approved pose, but only two small masked regions are ever composited. No generated full-body frames are swapped. One outstretched paw can also be separated with overlapping clip regions and rotated slightly behind the preserved forearm. These are limited layered actions, not a complete articulated puppet.

[MDN SVG masks](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/SVG/Reference/Element/mask) document local compositing; [Live2D material separation](https://docs.live2d.com/en/cubism-editor-manual/divide-the-material/) explains why independent parts need preparation. Both pages checked again on 19 October 2026. The choice to use eye patches is our project-specific inference, supported by the approved master greeting and this new 24-pose visual check, not a vendor guarantee. New eyes and motions remain In Review. See [implementation and evidence](../design/references/nexai/layered-poses-v1/README.md).

## Original recommendation — superseded

**For this request, animate each unchanged approved PNG with a short, individually tuned Web Animations API transform.** Keep one source image throughout each clip. Use restrained translation, rotation and uniform scale around a pose-specific support point, then return exactly to rest. This provides modest visual life without redrawing Nexai. It is a presentation effect, not a skeletal cat performance.

**For the highest-quality independent breathing, blinking, paws, ears and tail, the better long-term method is an artist-prepared layered/mesh rig.** Those layers do not exist for these 24 flattened illustrations. No credible universal method turns them into fully articulated approved puppets without preparation and visual review. The user's new acceptance of small movement makes the immediate solution appropriate; it does not invalidate the [previous rigging research](2026-10-18-nexai-animation-method.md).

The fit judgments in the last column are project-specific inferences, not vendor claims that a technique always succeeds or fails. There is no objectively best animation method independent of the desired motion and available source artwork.

## Motion direction for the immediate review

- Preserve every approved PNG and frozen hash. Use the [pose catalogue](../design/references/nexai/poses-v2/catalogue.json) as the identity register.
- Make 24 named clips, each retaining its own illustration. Vary their timing, pivot and small movement according to posture: seated settle, standing weight shift, lying rest, modest celebratory lift. Do not call a whole-image tilt a head turn, blink, paw wave or tail wag.
- Start conservatively: sub-degree rotations, tiny uniform scale changes, only a few display pixels of travel. These are proposed art-direction starting points, not scientifically established thresholds. Judge the result at its actual on-page size.
- Preserve proportions: no independent horizontal/vertical scaling, elastic squash, perspective flip, tremor, flashing or opacity flicker. Keep sad and angry poses restrained rather than adding distressing shaking.
- Ease in and out, start/end at the identical neutral transform, and leave visible rests between repeats. A finite clip should work by itself. A review loop must be explicitly controllable; do not run all 24 continuously by default.
- Reserve enough transparent/outer space for the full body throughout the maximum transformed bounds. Keep facial pixels crisp at the intended display size; inspect rather than assuming browser resampling looks good.
- Treat the collection as 24 separate poses, not frames of one motion. Switching poses is a semantic change, not a smooth transition. Do not crossfade overlapping faces as a substitute for articulation.

## Runtime safeguards

1. Decode an image before playing it. `HTMLImageElement.decode()` resolves when decoding is complete; on failure retain usable text and static fallback. [MDN decode](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLImageElement/decode)
2. Cancel the prior animation and scheduled replay on stop, switching pose or removal. Handle the expected `AbortError` from a cancelled `finished` promise. Rendering again must not count as a new semantic event. [MDN cancel](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Animation/cancel)
3. Suspend work when the document is hidden or the illustration leaves the viewport. Guard against late decode callbacks restarting cancelled motion. [Page visibility](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Document/visibilityState), [Intersection Observer](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Intersection_Observer_API)
4. Respect reduced motion at load and when the preference changes: show the unchanged static pose. Offer a visible stop/pause control for optional repeating motion. WCAG's pause/stop/hide criterion applies to qualifying automatically starting movement lasting more than five seconds alongside other content. [Reduced motion](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion), [W3C criterion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)
5. Animate only transform; avoid layout properties, per-frame image swaps, expensive blur and a permanent `will-change` on 24 images. The Chrome guidance specifically cautions against unnecessary layer promotion. Use browser performance inspection before adding such hints. [web.dev performance guide](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)
6. Keep motion decorative. Do not move keyboard focus or repeatedly announce it. Feedback text and educational mapping remain separate from the pose player.

## Verification and limits

Compare every clip with its static source; inspect start, peak and return at normal/small sizes. Test rapid replay, stop during decode, scrolling out of view, backgrounding, reduced motion, missing image and keyboard controls. Check no clipping/overflow at narrow and tablet layouts. Verify asset hashes independently of motion tests.

A local desktop browser test cannot establish physical Tab S9 Ultra smoothness. Test on that device before claiming device readiness. A finite transform animation also cannot promise the illusion of a breathing articulated cat; that is an explicit limit to show in review rather than hide behind the word “animation”.
